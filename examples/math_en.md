================================================================
 lat — Analysis through the structure of thinking patterns
================================================================

SOURCE PATTERN (the thought under examination)
------------------------------------------------
DE:  «For building the walls, the four children need 160 screws in
      total. They share the work and the screws fairly. How many
      screws must each child tighten?»

Arithmetic surface:  160 ÷ 4 = 40 screws per child.

Method: lat is used not for calculating but as a change of
lighting. The problem is forced through the grammar of other
languages. Each structure makes ONE silent assumption of the word
problem visible — precisely those assumptions that make the path
from the calculation to the "correct" answer seem straight and
without alternative.


----------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THE PROBLEM ALREADY PRESUPPOSES (the silent structures)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Before anyone calculates, the German text has silently decided four
things:

  1. "160 screws" and "the screws ... fairly" — screws count as
     IDENTICAL, countable, divisible units. "Fairly" is tacitly set
     as "equal number".                          -> Object boundaries
  2. "the four children ... each child" — the children count as
     interchangeable, equivalent divisors. Their number (4) is
     chosen precisely so that 160 divides evenly.
                                          -> Perspective/symmetry
  3. "must ... tighten" — the child is a consciously, deliberately
     acting agent; the tightening is controlled work.
                                             -> Agency & rank
  4. "For building ... need ... must" — the German causal/purpose
     grammar (purpose -> need -> obligation) frames everything as a
     seamless chain.                              -> Causality

The lenses below each pull on one of these four positings.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 1 — Yucatec Maya / classifier language
  Theme: Object boundaries
  Focus: Material instead of counting
  Feature: nouns count as unbounded material; individuation is
           expressed only through a classifier.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 1 — that "screw" is a self-evident unit.

In Yucatec one cannot simply say "160 screws". One needs a
classifier that first decides WHICH unit is cut out of the material
"screw-being" — by shape, position, function. Forced through this
grammar, the smooth number falls apart:

  - Is it 160 pieces in the box (objects),
  - or 160 bore-holes to be filled (tasks),
  - or 160 turning-actions (activities)?

INSIGHT: "Sharing fairly" is not at all unambiguous. Does one share
the PIECES fairly (each child gets 40 screws) or the WORK fairly
(each child turns for equally long / equally hard)? The German
sentence claims both are the same ("the work AND the screws
fairly") — but a screw in hard wood is not the same work as one in
soft wood. The classifier lays bare: the problem presupposes that
counting = fairness. That is a decision, not a fact.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 2 — Basque (Euskara) / purely ergative
  Theme: Causality / Agency & rank
  Focus: System state instead of authorship
  Feature: Only the deliberately acting agent takes the ergative;
           an involuntarily rolling stone is treated like an object.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 3 — the pure attribution of action.

German treats "need" and "tighten" grammatically almost alike (the
children as subject). Basque forces one to separate who REALLY acts
consciously:

  - "tighten" -> the child takes the ergative: clearly willed,
    controlled doing.
  - "the walls need 160 screws" -> here no one acts; this is a
    system state, not an act. The children here are not causers but
    are merely called upon by the building-need.

INSIGHT: The problem glues together a CONSTRAINT OF THINGS ("160
screws are needed") with an ACTION ("each child must turn"). The
ergative makes visible: the 160 are not the free act of the
children at all, but a number set from outside (walls, building
plan). The "fair" distribution thus distributes not self-chosen work
but an externally imposed need. The "must" is obligation, not
authorship.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 3 — Nez Percé (Nimipuutímt) / tripartite case system
  Theme: Agency & rank
  Focus: Absolute relational symmetry of all sentence parts
  Feature: marks intransitive subject, transitive subject, and
           transitive object with three DIFFERENT forms.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 2 — the silent equating "fair = equal".

Nez Percé cannot unnoticed equate "to be/exist" (intransitive) with
"to act on something" (transitive) — it has three separate cases.
Applied to the problem, it cleanly separates three roles that German
fuses:

  - the child that IS THERE (one of four),
  - the child that TURNS (agent),
  - the screw that IS TURNED (patient).

And precisely this compelled symmetry is the deep structure of
"fairness": 40 = 40 = 40 = 40. The mathematics of the problem IS the
grammatical symmetry that Nez Percé demands — each of the four
positions marked exactly equal.

INSIGHT: "Fair" means in the problem: relational symmetry between
interchangeable units. That is elegant, but it is only ONE fairness
— that of equal parts. As soon as one child is smaller, younger, or
less practiced (intransitively different), the symmetry breaks, and
"fair" would have to mean something other than "equally many". The
language shows: the problem chooses the simplest of several possible
fairnesses.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 4 — Cherokee (Tsalagi) / classificatory action verbs
  Theme: Object boundaries
  Focus: Physical geometry and consistency in space
  Feature: no abstract "give"/"have"; the verb changes according to
           whether the object is liquid, long-rigid, living,
           rope-like, or a lump.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Brings back what the number abstracts away — the body of the thing.

"Tighten" is in German an abstract verb that holds for everything.
Cherokee does not permit this: a screw is a LONG-RIGID, turnable
object, and the action verb would have to carry exactly this
geometry. This brings into view what "tighten 40 screws" bodily
means — 40 times setting to, gripping, turning, feeling resistance.

INSIGHT: The word problem turns a physical, enduring, tiring activity
into a pure number (40). The Cherokee verb refuses to let the object
and its handling drop away. It reminds us that "shared fairly" means
for one child: 40 real turnings on a long-rigid thing — not an
abstract share. The mathematization is powerful because it forgets
the geometry; the language brings it back.


----------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRAST — why German was set aside
----------------------------------------------------------------
The source language is German (fusional, with a reflexive pull toward
the causal and purpose chain: "For building ... need ... must"). Its
basic reflex is to present the situation as a seamless chain purpose
-> need -> obligation -> calculation, and in doing so to presuppose
screws and children as smooth, equal, divisible units. That is
precisely why German was excluded from the search: the four lenses lay
bare what the German problem text supposes as self-evident — the unit
of the "screw" (Yucatec Maya), the separation of constraint and act
(Basque), the choice of ONE fairness (Nez Percé), and the lost
physical body of the work (Cherokee).


================================================================
 SYNTHESIS
================================================================
Filtered through the four structures, the seemingly banal calculation
opens up:

  • Yucatec Maya -> "fair" is ambiguous: equal PIECES or equal WORK?
    The problem equates the two; that is a decision, not a fact.

  • Basque       -> the 160 are a need set from outside, not an act
    of the children. The "must" is obligation, not authorship.

  • Nez Percé    -> "fair = equal" is relational symmetry between
    interchangeable units — elegant, but only ONE of several possible
    fairnesses (it breaks as soon as the children differ).

  • Cherokee     -> the number 40 conceals 40 real turnings on a
    long-rigid thing; the mathematics is powerful because it forgets
    the geometry of the work.

Most useful reframing: The problem is usually read as a pure division
exercise (160 ÷ 4 = 40). The lenses turn it into an exercise about the
PRICE of abstraction: for 160 ÷ 4 to come out smooth and "fair",
screws had to be made into identical units, children into
interchangeable divisors, and work into a number. The calculation is
correct — but each of these three equatings was a silent positing that
another language does not go along with.
