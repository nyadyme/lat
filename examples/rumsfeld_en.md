SOURCE PATTERN (the thought under examination)
------------------------------------------------
EN:  "Reports that say that something hasn't happened are always
      interesting to me, because as we know, there are known
      knowns; there are things we know we know. We also know there
      are known unknowns; that is to say we know there are some
      things we do not know. But there are also unknown unknowns—
      the ones we don't know we don't know. And if one looks
      throughout the history of our country and other free
      countries, it is the latter category that tends to be the
      difficult ones."

Method: lat is used as a change of lighting. Unlike with
advertising- or campaign-rhetorical texts, there is here a REAL
epistemological order — often mocked, but sound at its core. The
four lenses therefore do not expose it but deepen and complete it.


----------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THE TEXT ALREADY DOES (the silent structures)
----------------------------------------------------------------
The text builds a taxonomy of knowledge and in doing so silently
does three things:

  1. It sorts knowledge by DEGREE OF CERTAINTY (known/unknown) — a
     grammatical concern that many languages compel.
                                          -> Evidence & certainty
  2. It nests knowledge about knowledge ("we know we know") —
     RECURSIVE meta-levels.                   -> Recursion/nesting
  3. It claims a grid with THREE fields — and leaves exactly a
     fourth silently out: the "unknown knowns" (what we know
     without knowing that we know it — the silent, embodied
     knowledge).                                 -> Logic/gap

Core point: The taxonomy is correct, but (a) elsewhere mere grammar,
(b) more deeply recursive than it admits, and (c) incomplete — it
lacks the fourth, precisely the most interesting field.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 1 — Tuyuca / obligatory evidentiality
  Theme: Evidence & certainty
  Focus: Verifiable origin of the information
  Feature: no sentence without one of five endings for the source
           — seen, heard, inference, hearsay, assumption.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Illuminates assumption 1 — the degree of certainty.

What Rumsfeld presents as a remarkable insight is in Tuyuca (Amazon)
simply obligatory grammar: NO sentence without a marking of its
source of knowledge. Where Rumsfeld opens three coarse boxes, Tuyuca
demands in EVERY sentence one of five gradations:

  - seen        -> "known known" from one's own viewing
  - heard       -> knowledge from another's voice
  - inference   -> "known unknown" that one deduces
  - hearsay     -> report without one's own checking
  - assumption  -> conjecture, openly marked as such

INSIGHT: Tuyuca shows two things. First, Rumsfeld's "insight" is
elsewhere everyday — some languages cannot even speak without
disclosing the source of knowledge. Second, his grid is COARSER: he
distinguishes only known/unknown, but Tuyuca FIVE origins. The famous
sentence is thus no peak of epistemology, but a first, coarse
approximation to what Tucanoan grammar accomplishes in every
subordinate clause.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 2 — Frame narrative / recursive nesting
  Theme: Evidence & certainty
  Focus: Recursive nesting of meta-levels
  Feature: a frame story encloses an inner one, which in turn
           produces a further one (One Thousand and One Nights).
----------------------------------------------------------------
Illuminates assumption 2 — the "we know that we know".

The text is built recursively: knowledge is nested within knowledge
("we know we know", "we don't know we don't know"). The frame
narrative makes these levels explicit as frames lying within one
another:

  Level 0: the state of affairs (something has / hasn't happened).
  Level 1: do we know it?          (known / unknown)
  Level 2: do we know whether we know it?   (Rumsfeld's point)
  Level 3: ... and do we know THAT? (the frame around the frame)

INSIGHT: The frame narrative shows that the recursion does not end at
level 2. Rumsfeld stops at "we don't know that we don't know" — but
the nesting runs on in principle (a frame around every frame).
Precisely in this nesting logic sits also the omitted fourth field:
lay the fourth frame — "we do NOT know that we DO know" — and the
silent knowledge that Rumsfeld leaves out appears. The form proves:
the grid was arbitrarily cut off at three.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 3 — Plato's Allegory of the Cave / epistemic level shift
  Theme: Evidence & certainty
  Focus: Epistemic level shift
  Feature: three-stage — shadow-paradise (deception), ascent
           (insight/pain), return (incomprehension of the crowd).
----------------------------------------------------------------
Illuminates the philosophical depth behind the taxonomy.

The Allegory of the Cave reads Rumsfeld's categories as positions
before the light:

  - "known knowns"    -> the shadows we see and name clearly (the
    familiar cave wall).
  - "known unknowns"  -> we sense that behind us a fire burns — we
    know of a beyond.
  - "unknown unknowns" -> the world OUTSIDE the cave, of which the
    chained do not even suspect that it exists.

INSIGHT: The allegory adds what Rumsfeld leaves open: whether one can
LEAVE the cave at all. His grid assumes the "unknown unknowns" are in
principle findable (one need only look carefully enough). Plato poses
the harder question: some ignorance is STRUCTURAL — bound to the
chaining, not to lack of attention. And the "ascent" is painful, the
"return" meets incomprehension: whoever sees the unknown unknowns is
rarely believed. That is the political bitterness behind the
matter-of-fact tone.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 4 — Koan / Zen paradox
  Theme: Logic & ambiguity
  Focus: Short-circuiting of the logical-analytical mind
  Feature: a rationally unsolvable paradox ("the sound of one hand
           clapping").
----------------------------------------------------------------
Illuminates assumption 3 — the omitted fourth field.

Rumsfeld's grid has logically four fields (known/unknown ×
conscious/unconscious), but he names only three. The missing fourth —
the "unknown knowns", i.e. what we know WITHOUT knowing that we know
it — is precisely the terrain of the koan: embodied, silent,
unutterable knowledge.

  Rumsfeld logic:  "What is the rule I follow without being able to
                    name it?"
  Koan:            unanswerable in the true/false mode; reachable
                   only as intuitive insight.

INSIGHT: The koan brings back precisely the field that the analytical
enumeration cannot grasp. "Unknown knowns" are no knowledge deficit
but implicit possession (skill, habit, prejudice, culture). They
cannot be raised by "looking more carefully" — only by a break of
routine, such as the koan compels. Rumsfeld's absence of this field
is no accident: it is the only one that cannot be reached through more
intelligence reports — and therefore remains invisible within the
security-policy frame.


----------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRAST — why English was set aside
----------------------------------------------------------------
The source language is English (analytic, WITHOUT grammatical
evidentiality: "we know", "we don't know" stand in the same plain
indicative that does not mark degrees of certainty). Precisely
because English compels no source of knowledge, Rumsfeld MUST rebuild
the distinction laboriously by paraphrase ("known", "unknown", "we
know we know") — and does so incompletely. That is why English was
excluded from the search: the lenses show what English leaves optional
— the grammaticalized source (Tuyuca), the recursive levels (frame
narrative), the structural level shift (cave), and the unutterable
fourth field (koan).


================================================================
 SYNTHESIS
================================================================
Filtered through the four structures, the mocked sentence becomes a
serious but unfinished epistemology:

  • Tuyuca         -> Rumsfeld's "insight" is elsewhere mere
    obligatory grammar — and there finer (five sources instead of
    two categories).

  • Frame narrative -> the recursion does not end at three; a frame
    around the frame lays bare the omitted fourth field.

  • Allegory of the Cave -> the "unknown unknowns" could be STRUCTURAL
    (bound to the chaining), not merely still unchecked; and whoever
    sees them is rarely believed.

  • Koan           -> the missing fourth field, the "unknown knowns"
    (silent, embodied knowledge), is unenumerable — no reporting
    deficit but implicit possession.

Most useful reframing: Rumsfeld's tripartition is no hollow phrase but
a coarse, incomplete map of knowledge. Its decisive blank is the
fourth field — the "unknown knowns": assumptions, habits, and
prejudices that we have long "known" without ever having consciously
checked them. In the security-policy context it is precisely this that
is the most dangerous category — not the unknown unknowns, which one
addresses with more reconnaissance, but the unchecked prior knowledge
of one's own, which no reconnaissance ever brings into view. The
lenses thus supplement the sentence with exactly what it would have
needed most: the field it conceals.
