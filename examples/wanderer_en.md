================================================================
 lat — Analysis through the structure of thinking patterns
================================================================

SOURCE PATTERN (the thought under examination)
------------------------------------------------
DE:  "A wanderer first walks 5 kilometers straight ahead. Then he
      turns left and walks a further 3 kilometers. How far has he
      walked in total?"

Arithmetic surface:
  what is asked is the DISTANCE WALKED (path length):  5 + 3 = 8 km.
  NOT asked (but suggested by the text) is the straight-line
  distance:  sqrt(5^2 + 3^2) = sqrt(34) ~ 5.83 km.

Method: lat is used not for calculating but as a change of
lighting. Each foreign structure makes ONE silent assumption of the
problem visible — above all the assumptions that make the spatial
part ("ahead", "turn left") appear meaningful at all.


----------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THE PROBLEM ALREADY PRESUPPOSES (the silent structures)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Before calculating, the German text has silently set four things:

  1. "straight ahead" — there is an egocentric ahead, a viewpoint
     zero-point from which direction is measured.
                                          -> Space & orientation
  2. "turn left" — "left" is defined ONLY relative to the current
     gaze of the wanderer; no compass-direction reference.
                                          -> Space & orientation
  3. "first ... then" — a strict temporal sequence, at the same time
     narrated as a spatial forward movement (time = way forward).
                                                 -> Time & aspect
  4. "How far ... walked in total" — the decisive but ambiguous
     question: path length (8) or straight-line distance (5.83)? The
     entire direction information is IRRELEVANT for the path length.
                                             -> Object boundaries/sense

The lenses below each pull on one of these positings. The deep point:
the "ahead / turn left" is, for the question actually asked, a
diversionary maneuver.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 1 — Guugu Yimithirr / absolute geocentric orientation
  Theme: Space & orientation
  Focus: Cosmic instead of egocentric grasping of space
  Feature: no left/right/front/back; every spatial relation via
           compass directions (North/East/South/West).
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumptions 1 and 2 — the ego zero-point.

Guugu Yimithirr (North Australia) does not know "ahead" and "left" at
all. One cannot form the sentence in this language without first
fixing in which COMPASS DIRECTION the wanderer goes:

  - "He walks 5 km NORTH. Then he turns WEST and walks 3 km."  (if
     "ahead" = North)

INSIGHT: At once it stands out that "straight ahead" is empty — it
names no direction, only "in the direction of gaze". And "left"
receives a sense only through the preceding "ahead" (left = 90°
against the momentary direction of gaze). Guugu Yimithirr exposes the
problem doubly:
  (a) the directions are not determined at all (North? East?),
  (b) for the question "how far walked" the direction is entirely
      indifferent — 5 km are 5 km, no matter where.
The language that COMPELS direction shows most clearly that the
problem carries direction only as decoration.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 2 — Aymara / reversed time metaphor
  Theme: Time & aspect
  Focus: Reversed time metaphor; visibility as axis
  Feature: The future lies BEHIND the speaker (unseen), the past
           BEFORE him (known, visible).
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 3 — the equating "forward = further, into the
future".

German glues space and time: "first ... ahead ... then". Going
forward = advancing in time. Aymara reverses this metaphor: what is to
come (the not-yet-walked 3 km) would lie BEHIND the wanderer, because
unseen; the already-walked 5 km would lie BEFORE him, because known and
"visible".

INSIGHT: The text uses "ahead" in two irreconcilable meanings at once:
spatial (direction of movement) and temporal (what is coming). Aymara
separates them by reversing the temporal axis. Thereby it becomes
visible: the seemingly natural narration "first forward, then turn" is
a cultural space-time coupling, not a necessity. The pure question
(total distance) contains no time at all — only a sum.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 3 — Shaped Poem / Calligramme (form)
  Theme: Space & orientation / Object boundaries
  Focus: Fusion of semantics and typography
  Feature: The line lengths reproduce the physical SHAPE of the
           object discussed (egg, cross, gearwheel).
----------------------------------------------------------------
Makes visible the geometry that the running text conceals.

Forced as a calligramme, the path must appear as its own shape on the
paper — an "L":

        S T A R T
        |
        |  (5 km straight ahead)
        |
        +---------------  (3 km to the left)

INSIGHT: Only as a shape does the difference become graspable that the
text conceals: the LINE WALKED (the edge of the L) is 5 + 3 = 8 km
long. The DISTANCE from the start (the imagined diagonal of the L) is
sqrt(34) ~ 5.83 km. The running text lets both magnitudes blur in "how
far"; the visual form separates path (the edges) and displacement (the
diagonal) at once. The problem asks for the edges — the diagonal is the
trap.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 4 — Pirahã / radical grammatical immediacy
  Theme: Time & aspect
  Focus: Absolute empirical present
  Feature: no recursion, no abstract color words, NO number words.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks the whole word-problem form — the number itself.

Pirahã (Amazon) has no exact number words, only "few" and "many", and
restricts statements to the immediately experienceable. In Pirahã the
sentence is unformulable: there is no "5", no "3", no "in total". At
most there would remain:

  "He went far. He turned. He went a bit further."

INSIGHT: Pirahã lays bare that the whole problem is an act of
abstraction. "5 km", "3 km", "in total 8" do not exist in the walked
experience, but only in the counting head. The language reminds us:
the wanderer experiences effort, ground, a turn — not the number 8.
The mathematization is powerful because it translates the walking
entirely into addable units; a numberless language shows how much
falls away in doing so.


----------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRAST — why German was set aside
----------------------------------------------------------------
The source language is German (fusional, egocentric spatial deixis
"ahead/left", strict tense sequence "first/then"). Its basic reflex is
to narrate movement from the I-perspective and to couple space with
time ("forward" = further). That is precisely why German was excluded
from the search: the four lenses lay bare what the German problem text
supposes as self-evident — the ego zero-point (Guugu Yimithirr), the
space-time coupling (Aymara), the hidden geometry of path-vs.-straight-
line (calligramme), and the abstraction of counting itself (Pirahã).


================================================================
 SYNTHESIS
================================================================
Filtered through the four structures, it shows that the problem
conceals more than it asks:

  • Guugu Yimithirr -> "ahead / left" is egocentric and indeterminate
    — and for the question asked even entirely indifferent. Direction
    is pure decoration.

  • Aymara          -> "ahead" secretly carries space AND time at once;
    the pure total distance contains no time at all, only a sum.

  • Calligramme     -> the shape of the path (an L) at once separates
    path length (8 km, the edges) from straight-line distance (5.83
    km, the diagonal). The diagonal is the built-in trap.

  • Pirahã          -> "5", "3", "in total 8" exist only in the
    counting head, not in the walking. The number is a translation,
    not an experience.

Most useful reframing: The problem looks like a geometry/Pythagoras
problem but is a pure addition problem (8 km) with built-in
distraction. Everything spatial — "straight ahead", "turn left" —
serves only to offer the diagonal (5.83 km) as a pseudo-solution. The
lenses make the actual lesson visible: one must first recognize WHICH
magnitude is asked for (path walked, not distance) before calculating.
The correct answer is 8 km — and the whole direction part was a test of
whether one lets oneself be distracted.
