 lat — Analysis through the structure of thinking patterns
================================================================

SOURCE PATTERN (the thought under examination)
------------------------------------------------
ZH:  「商賈入市，賤買貴賣。積金盈室，期以致富。
      故曰：貪人之欲，奪天之公，必召其禍。」

EN:  "The merchant enters the market, buys cheap and sells
      dear. He piles up gold until it fills the house, and hopes
      thereby to grow rich. Therefore it is said: the greed of
      human desire robs the impartiality of Heaven (天之公) and
      inevitably summons its calamity."

Method: lat is used as a change of lighting. Each foreign
structure makes ONE silent assumption visible. The text is a moral
teaching; what is striking lies not in the market behavior but in
the CONCLUSION: from "buy cheap, sell dear" there arises, via 故
(therefore) and 必 (inevitably), an unavoidable cosmic law of
punishment.


----------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THE TEXT ALREADY PRESUPPOSES (the silent structures)
----------------------------------------------------------------
In a few characters the text performs a great leap that its
brevity smooths over:

  1. 賤買貴賣 ("buy cheap, sell dear") — first describes neutral
     market behavior.
  2. 奪天之公 ("robs the impartiality of Heaven") — reinterprets
     this behavior as a deliberate THEFT from a cosmic order.
                                             -> Agency & rank
  3. 故曰 ("therefore it is said") — the conclusion is expressly
     marked as a handed-down proverb.
                                          -> Evidence & certainty
  4. 必召其禍 ("inevitably summons its calamity") — a moral
     judgment becomes a NECESSARY law of nature.
                                          -> Causality / time

Core point: The text disguises a VALUE JUDGMENT ("greed is bad")
as an unavoidable CAUSAL LAW ("greed necessarily causes
calamity"). The lenses separate the two.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 1 — Basque (Euskara) / purely ergative
  Theme: Causality / Agency & rank
  Focus: System state instead of authorship
  Feature: Only the deliberately acting agent takes the ergative;
           an involuntarily rolling stone is treated like an
           object.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 2 — 奪 ("to rob") as a conscious act.

奪 ("to rob, to snatch away") ascribes to greed a deliberate,
controlled perpetration — as though the merchant consciously
plunders Heaven. Basque forces one to check who REALLY acts with
intent:

  - 賤買貴賣 (buy cheap, sell dear) -> market behavior; in Basque
    rather a system state than a directed act against someone.
  - 奪天之公 (rob Heaven of its impartiality) -> here an ergative
    would have to stand, asserting a clearly deliberate robbery.

INSIGHT: Basque exposes a leap from trade to theft. Buying and
selling is not deliberately directed against "Heaven"; only the
word 奪 imputes intent and a victim. Thus a relationless system
process (prices, supply, demand) becomes a morally charged act with
a perpetrator and a robbed party. The language that MUST mark
intent shows that the text reads the intent in only afterward.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 2 — Ithkuil / causal triage
  Theme: Logic & ambiguity / Causality
  Focus: Elimination of ambiguity; hyper-precise categories
  Feature: forces one to state whether a consequence is intended,
           unintended, PHYSICALLY COMPELLED, or psychologically
           conditioned.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 4 — the 必 ("inevitably").

必召其禍 claims the calamity follows NECESSARILY. Ithkuil permits
no vague necessity; it would have to be marked WHICH kind of
compulsion is meant:

  - The text's logic assumes: PHYSICALLY COMPELLED — like a law of
    nature ("greed -> calamity", as certain as cause/effect).
  - Actually available is only: MORALLY/PSYCHOLOGICALLY CONDITIONED
    — an ethical conviction that wrongdoing OUGHT to be punished.

INSIGHT: Ithkuil separates the "must" of physics from the "ought"
of morality. The text uses the grammar of natural necessity (必)
for a sentence that is in truth a moral obligation. There is no
named mechanism chaining greed to calamity — only the claim that it
is compulsory. A moral "ought" disguises itself as a cosmic "must".


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 3 — Nganasan / imaginative & hypothetical evidentials
  Theme: Evidence & certainty
  Focus: Linguistic separation of reality and simulation
  Feature: distinct moods mark whether something is real, dreamed,
           a hallucination, or a CONSCIOUS MYTH.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks assumption 3 — the 故曰 ("therefore it is said").

故曰 expressly marks the conclusion as a handed-down word — not as
one's own observation. Nganasan (Siberian) has dedicated moods for
exactly this distinction. The core sentence would have to be carried
in the mood of CONSCIOUS MYTH:

  - "buy cheap, sell dear, pile up gold" -> REAL, observed.
  - "greed robs Heaven and summons calamity" -> MYTH mood: a
    consciously transmitted moral teaching, not an empirical fact.

INSIGHT: Nganasan forces honesty about the kind of statement. The
teaching is valuable as culturally transmitted wisdom — but it is
no empirical causal report. The text places market observation
(real) and doctrine of punishment (myth) side by side in the same
tone; the 故曰 betrays that the second part stems from tradition,
not observation. Marked as myth, the statement loses nothing of its
dignity — but it loses its false claim to be a law of nature.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LENS 4 — Enumeration / coexistence instead of causality
  Theme: Causality / coexistence
  Focus: Coexistence instead of causality
  Feature: works without direction-asserting verbs; refuses the
           causal chain structurally.
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attacks the central movement — the 故 ... 必召 (arrow of cause).

One strikes out the directional verbs (故, 召, 必) and merely places
the states side by side:

  "Merchant in the market. Bought cheap, sold dear. Gold in the
   house. Wealth hoped for. Calamity — sometimes."

INSIGHT: Without the causal arrow, the compulsory chaining falls
away. Greed and calamity then merely lie beside one another, no
longer in a cause-effect order. And immediately it stands out: there
are rich merchants who remain untouched, and calamity that strikes
poor and rich alike. The "compulsory" connection was a moral
positing, not an observed regularity. Enumeration shows: succession
(first greed, then perhaps ruin) is not the same as causation.


----------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRAST — why Classical Chinese was set aside
----------------------------------------------------------------
The source language is Classical Chinese (Wényán): isolating,
without inflection or tense, a single character can be noun, verb,
or adjective — causality is NOT morphologically compelled there but
arises topologically out of parallelism and symmetry. That is
precisely why it is revealing that THIS text brushes its own
language against the grain: with 故 (therefore) and 必 (inevitably)
it builds in explicit causal and necessity markers that Wényán does
not itself require. That is why the source language was excluded from
the search — the four lenses lay bare what the text compels with
these superimposed markers: imputed intent (Basque), feigned
physical necessity (Ithkuil), handed-down myth in the guise of fact
(Nganasan), and an arrow of cause where there is only coexistence
(Enumeration).


================================================================
 SYNTHESIS
================================================================
Filtered through the four structures, a moral teaching shows itself
that passes itself off as a law of nature:

  • Basque      -> 奪 ("to rob") reads intent and a victim into a
    relationless market behavior; trade becomes theft from Heaven.

  • Ithkuil     -> 必 uses the grammar of natural necessity for a
    moral obligation; an "ought" disguises itself as a "must",
    without mechanism.

  • Nganasan    -> 故曰 betrays a handed-down teaching; the sentence
    of punishment belongs in the myth mood, not the fact mood.

  • Enumeration -> without the causal arrow, mere coexistence
    remains; greed and calamity follow one another sometimes, but
    do not necessarily cause one another.

Most useful reframing: The sentence is a textbook case of how
language transforms a VALUE JUDGMENT into a CAUSAL LAW. That greed
is bad is an ethical conviction; that greed INEVITABLY summons
calamity is an empirical claim that the text does not prove but
grammatically compels through 故 and 必. Both are legitimate — but
they are two different things. The wisdom of the teaching (moderation,
distrust of avarice) stands; only its claim to be an unavoidable
cosmic law of punishment dissolves under the lenses. Tellingly, to
do this the text even had to overwrite its own naturally
parallel-coexistent language with superimposed causal markers.
